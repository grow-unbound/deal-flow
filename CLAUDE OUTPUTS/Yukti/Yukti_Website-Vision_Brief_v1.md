## HOME

### 1. Hero

**Eyebrow (mono, uppercase):** THE OPERATING LAYER OF THE BUSINESS

**H1:** Run your whole business in one place — and grow it.

**Sub:** Yukti captures the work that actually drives your business — buying, selling, pricing, customers, fulfilment — and turns it into the clarity and momentum you need to grow. You make the calls. Yukti turns them into growth.

**CTAs:** [Book a demo] (copper) · [How it works] (charcoal ghost)

**Visual:** two-tone keystone rendered large; beneath it, a single composed product frame (cockpit + buyer phone) seated like a stone in the arch. Space is the luxury here.

**Tagline lockup near the mark:** *The keystone of your business.*

### 2. The enemy (manifesto section — text-led, no cards)

**H2:** Most software makes you serve the system.

Prose block (short lines, editorial setting):

> Feed it data. Configure it. Reconcile it. File through it.
> Somewhere along the way, the tool became the boss.
>
> We call this the administrative tax on growth — the busywork that sits between a business owner and the next good decision.
>
> Yukti inverts it. The system serves the operator. You decide; the platform carries the weight.

### 3. What "operating layer" means (the claim, made concrete)

**H2:** Built around what your business does — not what it has to file.

Three columns:

1. **Capture the real work.** Prices decided, campaigns launched, orders taken, goods dispatched — recorded where they happen, the moment they happen. Once.
2. **See the whole arch.** One picture of customers, catalog, pricing, and orders. No re-entry, no reconciliation between five tools, no version of the truth per portal.
3. **Turn decisions into growth.** Yukti shows you what needs attention and what to do next — the right move, surfaced. You stay in command.

**Precise-register footnote line (small, deliberate):** And because everything is captured once and structured, your books stay clean — data flows to the tools your accountant already uses.

### 4. The keystone story (brand section)

**H2:** Why the keystone?

> Every business is an arch under load. The owner lays the stones — what they buy, sell, price, and promise. An arch only stands when the keystone drops into place: the one piece that turns loose stones into a structure that bears weight and rises. Yukti is that keystone.

Visual: the voussoir mark drawn as a diagram — three stones, seam lines, load arrows. Structural, not decorative.

### 5. Where we begin (the vertical proof — this is what keeps the site honest)

**H2:** We start where the load is heaviest: distribution.

Copy: India's multibrand distributors move the country's goods across dozens of brands, hundreds of retailers, and thousands of prices — mostly on WhatsApp and memory. It's one of the hardest operating problems in commerce, and nobody built for the distributor. So we did.

Three proof tiles (from the shipped product):
- **Campaigns** — the right price, for the right retailers, for a limited time. One flow.
- **WhatsApp, with a memory** — every retailer reached individually; sent, delivered, opened, ordered — tracked.
- **Orders without leakage** — from enquiry to delivered, in one system your team and your buyers share.

CTA: [Yukti for distributors →] /distribution

**Forward line (the expansion tease, calibrated):** Distribution is first. Manufacturers and other operators are next. The operating layer is the same; the work it carries changes.

### 6. Principles (how we build — doubles as hiring page bait)

**H2:** What we believe.

1. **The operator is the hero.** Yukti recommends; the owner decides. Software that makes its user powerful beats software that makes itself indispensable.
2. **Capture once.** Data entered twice is a tax. Work recorded at the moment it happens is an asset.
3. **Bold about your growth. Exact about your money.** We'll be ambitious about what you can win, and conservative about what we promise on data and rupees.
4. **Respect the professionals.** Your accountant's judgment is irreplaceable. Their data entry isn't. We build for that distinction.
5. **India-first, world-ready.** Built for lakh and crore, GST and WhatsApp — architected to travel.

### 7. Final CTA

**H2:** Put the keystone in.

Copy: See your own business running on Yukti — bring a price list, leave with a live campaign on your phone.

## /HOW-IT-WORKS

Concrete walk-through so the vision never floats free. Four scenes, each a screenshot + short copy, told as one distributor's Tuesday:

1. **9:00 — Decide.** A new stock arrives. Build a campaign: products, campaign price, retailer group, valid one week. The builder shows the average discount before you commit.
2. **9:07 — Reach.** The campaign goes to 84 retailers' WhatsApp — individually, tracked, opt-out respected. Not a group blast; a personal price list each.
3. **All day — Capture.** Retailers order from their phones. No install, no passwords. Orders land in one queue with statuses, not in seventeen chats.
4. **6:00 — Know.** The funnel: 84 sent → 81 delivered → 52 opened → 19 ordered. Tomorrow's call list writes itself. Orders flow to Tally/Zoho; the books stay clean.

Close: **That's the operating layer. One day, one system, zero re-entry.** [Book a demo]

## Copy rules (Option B-specific, in addition to Option A's list)

1. The category term appears as **"the operating layer of the business"** — never "operating system," never "OS," never "ERP," never "all-in-one accounting."
2. Finance mentions are footnote-register: always *outcome for the owner + respect for the accountant*, never architecture ("ledger as a view," "accounting as by-product" are internal-only).
3. Every abstract section must be followed within one scroll by something concrete (product frame, the Tuesday story, a distributor proof tile). Vision floats; proof anchors.
4. No roadmap specifics beyond "manufacturers and other operators are next." No payments, credit, AI, or accounting-absorption hints.
5. The manifesto section is the only place allowed to be editorial/long-line; everywhere else, Option A's economy of copy applies.
